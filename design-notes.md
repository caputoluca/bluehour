# Blue Hour — design notes

The README says what the theme is; the changelog says what shipped when. This is the record: where the palette came from, what each version changed, what was tried and rejected, and the sources behind the ladder. Values are kept here so that nothing gets rebuilt twice.

## Where the palette came from

The hues came from a room photographed at blue hour: the sofa's blue became the keyword accent, the lamp's peach became the strings, and the floor became the near-black ground. The values did not come from the photographs. Sampled paint and fabric are low in chroma and carry casts (v1 below), so every shipped value was designed on a lightness ladder in OKLCH and checked against the ground for WCAG ratio and APCA contrast. The ladder table in the README is the result; `palette.json` holds the same values under the same names.

## The versions

Four were built in one evening and a fifth the next, all on the same ten sample files. The dead ones were deleted and the reasons kept.

1. **v1 — the room's swatches on a monochrome scheme.** Read flat: three near-whites within a hair of one another, and the sampled near-black carried a blue cast. Photographed paint and fabric are low in chroma; on a screen beside Dark+ they wash out.
2. **v2 — Dark+'s role structure with the nearest swatch for each role.** Read as colours thrown together; baby-blue variables were the clearest case of a swatch on a word for no reason. A mapping is not a design.
3. **v3 — the blueprint done properly: six full hues on a designed ladder.** Reading became easy and the theme became generic, because six full hues is Dark+'s own recipe.
4. **v4 — the same ladder, two accent families, flat near-black.** The first version worth keeping. Shipped as 0.1.0 for a trial.
5. **v5 — names up a rung, calls onto the sky, the frame lifted (0.2.0), then the ground tinted (0.2.1).** The 0.1.0 trial found it clean but hard to read, and it was unclear whether that was habituation. Probes on the same file settled it: Vesper (two hues, white text, Lc 107) was also hard, GitHub Dark read easily, One Dark Pro was too busy. Contrast did not track the verdicts; hue count did, and a new many-hue theme reading fine on first sight ruled out habituation. I read by hue, and the 0.1.0 line gave the eye no cue to find. The published themes corrected the first fix: four of the seven read for this design leave variables plain and colour the functions instead. Names moved up a rung, from `#C4C4C4` (Lc 70, under the body-text floor) to Dark+'s own `#D4D4D4` (Lc 80). Tried on names first and rejected: lavender (slightly easier to read, wrong for a room with no purple), tan, cream, and the ladder's top white, which jarred when scrolling. Calls moved from white to the sky, shared with types as Vesper and GitHub Dark do. That read as too much blue, so two ways out were tried and rejected: keywords to a neutral (Vesper's move) and calls to the apricot. Calls on the sky stayed, because blue is the theme's main colour. Frame text (explorer, inactive tabs, breadcrumbs) had sat far below Dark+ (Lc 43 and 21 against about 75 and 40) and came up; carets and the activity-bar badge lost the peach, so warm in the frame now means attention only: warnings, ANSI yellow, a prompt. The ground came last, as 0.2.1: a lightness step of +0.03 L on its own was invisible; the real complaint was that zero chroma reads as lifeless, so the three bottom rungs took a trace of the blue-hour hue at the same lightness (`#181B1F` / `#212428` / `#2F3236`, C 0.009, H 256), the v1 cast done on the ladder instead of sampled. The status bar, section headers and titles went from the comment rung to the explorer rung at the same time. Two hybrids were built and shown, this frame with Dark+'s code and with GitHub Dark's: both read bright and too dark at once on `#141414`, a ground darker than either syntax set was designed for. Deleted; the lesson was the ground, not the hybrids.
6. **v6 — the ground back to black (0.2.2), and the chroma question (0.2.3).** A day on each, side by side: the 0.2.1 tinted ground read too blue and the black grew on me, while the faded feeling stayed on the black. That located it in the accents. The three bottom rungs went back to `#141414` / `#1D1D1D` / `#2B2B2B`; the 0.2.1 frame-text lift stayed. Shipped as 0.2.2. The cool accents run at about half of Dark+'s chroma and a third of GitHub Dark's (keywords C 0.05 against 0.11 and 0.16; strings at 0.085 match both), every accent is a pastel, and calls sit within 0.03 L of plain names, so only a weak hue separates a call from a name. Tested as 0.2.3: the four accents at the same lightness and 1.4 times the chroma (`#8EA7C4` → `#84A8D0`, `#A9D1EA` → `#9AD3F6`, `#E6A68A` → `#F79E76`, `#ECCAAD` → `#F6C79E`; every Lc unchanged, so only the pop moved). It read like other themes, a blue theme instead of a monochrome one, and was reverted unreleased. So chroma is part of the identity, not only the hue count: the cool family covers most of the coloured tokens on a line, and lifting it makes a blue theme by a different road than v3's six hues. Third time the pattern held: pushed toward pop it reads generic, restrained it reads nice. Closed: the understated read is the charm and the faded feeling is its price. A 1.2 step was not tried, because the invisible 0.03 L ground step in v5 says small steps here read as nothing.
7. **v7 — Paper: a trace of warmth in the neutrals (0.2.4 and 0.2.5, withdrawn in 0.2.6).** The worry after locking the black and the low chroma was that cool, dark and desaturated might be a dispiriting thing to look at all day. Checked rather than guessed: Valdez and Mehrabian (1994) and Wilms and Oberfeld (2018) find brightness and saturation drive mood far more than hue, blue among the most pleasant hues, and dark plus desaturated the low-valence corner. So the effect is real, it comes from the ground and the chroma rather than the blue, and both had just been chosen on purpose. The cheapest counter, the move every warm dark theme makes, is the neutrals: the six text rungs from the caret white to the line numbers tinted toward hue 70, chroma under 0.01, every Lc unchanged, ground and accents untouched. At 0.010 it felt warmer and more approachable, but Markdown prose showed the tint about twice as strongly as code, because prose is a solid mass of one colour, and read orange. Halved to 0.006 and shipped as 0.2.4. A table of twenty dark themes through the checker showed the hue was wrong: every warm-text theme (Gruvbox, Kanagawa, Flexoki, Ayu, Zenburn) sits at hue 80 to 107, paper yellow, while this tint rounded to hue 60, the orange edge; only Dark+ and Vesper ship untinted gray. Shifted to hue 95 at the same chroma as 0.2.5: `#E9E8E4`, `#D5D4D0`, `#C5C4C0`, `#93928E`, `#81807C`, `#64635F`, every Lc unchanged. Both were packaged as separate themes so the picker flipped the same file live with the gray build beside it. The tint read as a weak yellow filter over everything, like Night Shift at noon, most visible in the terminal, whose ANSI whites carry it; the gray read sharper and more of a statement; and after a while the two were hard to tell apart, which is the eye adapting a uniform cast away within minutes. The first call had gone to warm, on the argument that how it reads all day outweighs how it looks as a statement; it did not survive the hour. The worry had been a projection from two papers, while the black had a day of use behind it, and observation beats projection. Gray is the lock. Warmth in the evening is the operating system's job: Night Shift on a sunset schedule is the same cast, every app, only at those hours. The same table answered why not cool and brighter text, like GitHub Dark: cool text at hue 250 sits 14 degrees from the calls' sky, so the name-to-call cue nearly vanishes (warm is 141 degrees away); brighter text had already lost in v5; and cool plus blue is the 0.2.3 verdict by another road. Shipped as 0.2.6: the 0.2.2 ladder.
8. **v8 — the terminal chrome; no version.** Two days into the trial the faded feeling had a location: not the editor but the Claude Code terminal, its spark and its mode line, the two things looked at most of the day. Both are painted from the theme's ANSI slots, because the tool's `dark-ansi` theme reads them: the spark is the bright-red slot, which the theme derives as a lighter rung of the error red, so the spark had been a pale error red by the tool's mapping rather than the theme's choice; the mode line is the tool's warning colour, the yellow slot, the gold. The tool's own palette paints them at chroma 0.13 and about 0.17; the slots deliver 0.10 and 0.055. Tried live, two slots, no rebuild: the lamp hue at 1.4 times (no visible change); the lamp hue two rungs darker at full chroma (orange, off the theme); the original hue pushed in four steps to the sRGB edge, each step a rung darker to buy chroma, since at that hue the screen cannot be bright and saturated at once (the last step read as plain red); then the 0.2.3 values (a step backwards). Fifth time the pattern held, now on two glyphs: under about 1.4 times reads as nothing, 2 times reads as off-theme, and there is no rung between. The other route: Claude Code reads a custom theme from `~/.claude/themes/`, a base plus per-key overrides; one built on `dark-ansi` with only the spark overridden to the tool's own terracotta looked the same. Closed: the slots stay as designed, and the faded feeling in the terminal is the same price the editor pays. Kept from it: the spark has its own key there, so if it ever matters it gets its own colour without touching the bright-red slot every other tool shares.
9. **v9 — the trial review; the active tab gets a line (0.2.7).** Seventeen days on real files, reviewed: a keep. The little code read fine, and the cool-and-dark feeling never arrived, which is what v7 predicted once the eye adapts the cast away; Night Shift and Paper close with the trial. Three notes from the trial went to the review; the next section has them. One change shipped: the active tab's 1px line lifted from the hairline rung to the line-numbers rung. Method for every candidate: `workbench.colorCustomizations` scoped to `[Blue Hour]`, one change at a time after a first pair confounded the verdict, flipped live with no rebuild, and the settings file restored to its previous bytes once 0.2.7 was installed.
10. **v10 — the bright yellow onto the ladder (0.2.8).** `gold_bright` had sat at hue 69 against the gold's 62 since 0.1.0, the one bright that did not hold its base's hue. Moved to `#FFDEC1`, the gold at L +0.06 (L 0.92, C 0.053, H 63, Lc 89.5). Shown live in the terminal, each next to the gold, the old and the new pair read the same, so the change is for the rule and not the eye: every bright is now its base one rung up, hue within a degree.

## What the trial settled

- **Which tab is active was hard to see.** The only cue separating active from inactive was the label: `tab.activeForeground` `#C4C4C4` (L 0.82, Lc 70) against `#929292` (L 0.66, Lc 43), a 0.16 L step, with both tab backgrounds the ground by the flat-ground rule. A git decoration then replaced the label colour and outranked it: modified is the gold at L 0.86, brighter than the active rung, so a modified background tab read brighter than the undecorated active one; untracked is the green at L 0.76, between the two, so it read as neither. And `tab.activeBorderTop` sat at the hairline rung, the same value as every border between tabs, so there was no marker to fall back on. The explorer already had the cue the tab bar lacked, `highlight_low`, a background block a git colour cannot erase (the gold still reads Lc 74.5 on it). Checked against six themes' own files (Dark+, Dark Modern, GitHub Dark, One Dark Pro, Vitesse, Vesper): every one marks the active tab with a line or a lifted block, never by label lightness alone; five draw a 1px line, three in their accent and two neutral; both flat-ground themes draw one, Vitesse a neutral line in its text colour, and Vesper's 0.03 L block lift is the step v5 found invisible. VS Code draws the line at 1px fixed, so a thicker hairline was never an option. Tried live, one flip at a time: the explorer block as the tab background, shown together with git-modified on the blue, read as too much blue and too loud for the theme; the line at the frame-text rung `#C4C4C4` popped too much; the line at the line-numbers rung `#636363` read slightly warm and worked. It is zero chroma; the warmth is simultaneous contrast against the blue beside it. A trace of the blue-hour hue on the line was not tried, because the same trace on the whole ground had read as nothing. Shipped in 0.2.7.
- **A problem read as a modified file.** Warnings and git-modified were one hex, the gold; errors and git-deleted were likewise one red. The theme also disagreed with itself on "modified": the gutter bar is the blue, the file name gold. Candidate: git-modified onto the blue `#8EA7C4` (L 0.72, C 0.051, H 252, WCAG 7.4:1, Lc 53), so gutter and file name would share one colour for changed and gold would mean warning only; it would also put a modified tab under the active rung instead of above it. Shown live, it read as too much blue. Of the six themes checked, four keep modified and warning in the same warm family and lean on VS Code's default gap (modified a tan `#E2C08D`, L 0.83, C 0.08, against a warning yellow `#CCA700`, L 0.74, C 0.15); only Vitesse moves modified to blue; Blue Hour is the only one where the two are one hex. The gap done on the ladder, modified one rung quieter at `#C4AC98` (L 0.76, C 0.04, Lc 59), would put changed files below plain file names, so they would recede, and the chroma cap means the difference cannot be bought back with saturation the way GitHub Dark does. Left as is: the M badge against a problem count carries the distinction. Reopen if the confusion recurs.
- **The Claude Code status line reads plain without a countdown.** Not the theme's to fix: that line is painted by a script in truecolor escapes, not from the ANSI slots, so the theme cannot reach it. Its colours were sampled from the room's photographs before the ladder existed; putting them on the ladder belongs with the script.

## Tried and withdrawn, so nobody rebuilds them

- **0.2.1, the tinted ground:** `#181B1F` / `#212428` / `#2F3236` (the three bottom rungs +0.03 L, C 0.009, H 256). Too blue after a day; the lift alone was invisible.
- **0.2.3, the accents at 1.4× chroma:** `#84A8D0`, `#9AD3F6`, `#F79E76`, `#F6C79E`. A blue theme.
- **0.2.4 and 0.2.5, Paper:** the six neutral text rungs at hue 70, then hue 95, chroma 0.006. A yellow filter; the eye adapts it away.
- **Two hybrids:** this frame with Dark+'s code, and with GitHub Dark's code. Both read bright and too dark at once on `#141414`, a ground darker than either syntax set was designed for; the lesson was the ground, not the hybrids.
- **Two chrome-only fallbacks:** Dark+'s code with this frame, and Dark+ with only the status bar changed. Both read fine, and not different enough to switch.
- **The terminal chrome variants** under v8: two slots at 1.4×, at full chroma two rungs down, pushed to the sRGB edge, and a Claude Code custom theme with the spark overridden.

## Sources

The ladder:

- Björn Ottosson, *A perceptual color space for image processing* (Oklab), 2020. https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/ — L is perceived lightness; the ladder is stepped in it, not in HSL.
- CSS Color Module Level 4, § Oklab and Oklch. https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/#ok-lab
- APCA, *APCA in a Nutshell*. https://github.com/Myndex/SAPC-APCA/blob/master/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.md — the Lc targets: 90 preferred for body text, 75 the minimum for it, 60 for other content text, 45 for large text, 30 for placeholders; and why WCAG's ratio is the wrong guide for dark mode.
- WCAG 2.2, 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum). https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum — every rung from `comment` up also clears 4.5:1; `muted` (line numbers, placeholders, hints) sits at 3.1:1 on purpose, as frame state read second.
- Material Design 2, *Dark theme*. https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html — saturated colours vibrate on dark surfaces; accents belong in the lighter tones.

Mood, for v7:

- Valdez, P., and Mehrabian, A. (1994). *Effects of color on emotions.* Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 123(4), 394–409. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.123.4.394
- Wilms, L., and Oberfeld, D. (2018). *Color and emotion: effects of hue, saturation, and brightness.* Psychological Research, 82(5), 896–914. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-017-0880-8

Whether highlighting helps reading at all, for v5:

- Sarkar, A. (2015). *The impact of syntax colouring on program comprehension.* PPIG 26. https://ppig.org/files/2015-PPIG-26th-Sarkar1.pdf — a small, significant gain that shrinks with experience.
- Hannebauer, C., Hesenius, M., and Gruhn, V. (2018). *Does syntax highlighting help programming novices?* Empirical Software Engineering 23. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-017-9579-0 — no evidence it does, at n = 390.
- Beelders, T., and du Plessis, J.-P. (2016). *Syntax highlighting as an influencing factor when reading and comprehending source code.* Journal of Eye Movement Research 9(1). https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/2429
- Dimitri, G. (2015). *The impact of syntax highlighting in Sonic Pi.* PPIG 26. https://www.ppig.org/files/2015-PPIG-26th-Dimitri.pdf
- Healey, C. (1996). *Choosing effective colours for data visualization.* IEEE Visualization. https://healey.csc.ncsu.edu/publications/15843.pdf — about seven distinguishable colours at once, in a search task, not code.

The published themes read for their role mappings (which words get a hue, which stay neutral):

- Catppuccin style guide. https://github.com/catppuccin/catppuccin/blob/main/docs/style-guide.md
- Nord, colours and palettes. https://www.nordtheme.com/docs/colors-and-palettes
- Solarized. https://ethanschoonover.com/solarized/
- Dracula spec. https://spec.draculatheme.com/
- Rosé Pine palette. https://github.com/rose-pine/palette — the neutral rung names `muted`, `subtle`, `text` that `palette.json` extends.
- Alabaster, and Nikita Prokopov's essay on syntax highlighting. https://github.com/tonsky/vscode-theme-alabaster · https://tonsky.me/blog/syntax-highlighting/
- VS Code's own Dark+ and Dark Modern theme files, in the microsoft/vscode repository.
